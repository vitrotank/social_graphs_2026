ONE QUESTION / TWO KINDS OF CLOSENESS

303 FROZEN PAGES · 45,753 PAIRS

Does shared vocabulary
need a short route?

A Wikipedia link records an editor’s reference. Shared vocabulary records what two pages spend words on. We wanted to know how often those two forms of closeness agree.

For each of the 303 Marvel articles, we choose the other article with the highest TF-IDF cosine similarity. We reuse Week 6’s title-word removal, so a repeated character title cannot take all the credit. Then we count the shortest route between the pages, allowing each frozen hyperlink to be followed in either direction.

Our comparison is every possible choice of another page: 91,506 source–target choices, with each source equally represented. A reciprocal nearest-neighbor match counts as two choices, because each page makes its own selection.

WHAT “SOUND ALIKE” MEANS HERE

Cosine between bags of weighted words. A rare shared word contributes more than a common one.

This measures lexical resemblance. Word order, negation and paraphrases can escape it; a dense embedding could tell a different story.

WHAT SURPRISED US

The closest words lead to a direct link for 194 pages. That agreement is striking beside the all-alternatives comparison. But 11 choices take four or more steps, and 21 have no route in this roster. A high cosine deserves a reading before it becomes a relationship.

THE MEASUREMENT / AND ITS LIMIT

Shared words.
Partial map.

Our answer is conditional on this representation: the closest vocabulary usually points to a nearby page, but it can also recover shared teams where a short hyperlink route is missing, or mistake a shared first name for a connection. Reading the passages is what separates those explanations.

The main limitation is the map. Routes stay inside this 303-page roster. Two articles with no path here could connect through a team, event or character page outside the sample. A missing path does not establish that the characters never meet. We also remove hyperlink direction to measure separation; this does not test whether a reader can click the same route forward.

Seventeen pages are completely isolated in the frozen graph. Their textual-neighbor choices must have no path, whatever their scores. The 21 disconnected choices include these unavoidable cases.

Title masking is a sensitivity check, not complete name removal: civilian names and aliases may remain, while ordinary words can disappear. Raw term counts can give a repeated passage considerable weight. Neither a cosine score nor this descriptive comparison proves that text causes links.

Reproduce the analysis and inspect the definitions

We tokenize the frozen articles into Unicode letter words, case-fold them, remove terminal possessive 's, then apply the same global title-word mask to every document. Each remaining term receives raw count × ln(N / document frequency), followed by L2 normalization. Cosine is the dot product of those vectors; no stoplist or learned embedding is added.

All 45,753 unordered pairs are scored. A page’s highest-cosine other page is chosen with canonical Wikipedia identifier as the tie-break. Breadth-first search gives an exact shortest path in the undirected version of the frozen graph. Disconnected pairs have a null distance and no path; zero vectors, if any, have cosine zero.

Run python scripts/analyze_week7.py, then python scripts/build.py --output _site from the repository root. Both use the Python standard library and committed inputs. No API keys or model download are required.
