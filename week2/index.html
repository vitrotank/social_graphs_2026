01 / MODELS & EVIDENCE

START WITH THE QUESTION

A striking pattern.
But compared to what?

Spider-Man receives 106 links. Most pages receive far fewer. A model helps us ask which mechanisms could produce such an uneven distribution.

An Erdős–Rényi model places edges at random. Preferential attachment gives well-connected nodes a greater chance of attracting the next link. They are useful comparisons, but a curve that looks similar does not identify the process that created Wikipedia.

The orange curve below is measured from the frozen roster, among pages with at least one incoming link. The comparison curves are illustrative mathematical shapes. Their agreement with the data is not a fitted model or a significance test.

↳ READING THE FIGURE

CCDF means the fraction of pages with degree at least k. This figure conditions on positive in-degree; zero-degree pages still belong to the network.

Switch views to see how a guide line can look convincing while the sparse tail contains only a few characters.

Why a straight line does not establish a power law

Cumulative points reuse observations, so they are not independent. A least-squares line on log–log axes is not enough to establish a power law. A defensible claim requires a fitted range, goodness-of-fit assessment, and comparison with alternative distributions.

The guide line in this exhibit has a chosen slope. This repository does not provide a likelihood-ratio test or a fitted power-law uncertainty estimate, so we report neither a significance value nor a scale-free verdict.

02 / THE POPULARITY TRAP

FOLLOW A LINK, MEET A HUB

Your neighbours
get more calls.

Ignore arrow direction and count each pair once. For 250 of the 286 non-isolated characters, the mean degree of their neighbours exceeds their own degree.

A / CHOOSE A PAGE

Every page gets a vote.

9.47 mean degree, all 303 pages

Choose uniformly from the whole roster. Isolates count too. If you restrict the sample to non-isolates, the mean is 10.03.

B / FOLLOW AN EDGE

Hubs get more chances.

22.08 mean degree at an edge endpoint

Choose an undirected edge uniformly, then one of its endpoints. A node with 100 links can be reached in 100 ways.

C / CHECK EACH PAGE

Most feel the paradox.

87.4% of non-isolated pages

This fraction compares each character with their own neighbours. It is a different statistic from averaging over edge endpoints.

Spider-Man and Radian (Morituri) are the only pages whose undirected degree strictly exceeds that of every neighbour. Radian wins locally on the separate nine-page island. “Friends” here means linked Wikipedia pages, not fictional friendships.

The one-line proof · why sampling changes the average

A random edge endpoint reaches a degree-k page with probability proportional to k. For a graph with at least one edge:

Mean degree at an edge endpoint = ⟨k²⟩ / ⟨k⟩
                                = ⟨k⟩ + Var(k) / ⟨k⟩

The non-negative variance term explains the larger mean. It does not guarantee that every individual has neighbours with higher degree, or fix the percentage of characters experiencing the paradox.

03 / THE SHUFFLE TEST

WHAT DOES YOUR NULL KEEP?

Change the wiring.
Keep the sockets.

A degree-preserving shuffle keeps every node's number of neighbours while changing who those neighbours are. It asks whether a pattern goes beyond the degree sequence.

Two ways to randomize · and a common pitfall

A configuration model pairs degree “stubs” and may create self-loops or parallel edges. Removing those edges afterwards changes the degree sequence, so the result no longer preserves the quantity you intended to control.

Double-edge swaps can operate directly on simple graphs. They still require enough accepted swaps, multiple randomizations, and checks that the chain has explored adequately. A single shuffled graph is not an uncertainty interval.

Higher clustering than an appropriate null would support structure beyond degrees. Team membership is a possible explanation to test with additional labels; hyperlinks alone do not establish it.

04 / GROWING A NETWORK

A MECHANISM IS A HYPOTHESIS

The rich get
more hyperlinks?

Preferential attachment lets new nodes connect more often to existing hubs. It offers a mechanism for unequal degree, but it does not reproduce every feature of this roster.

01 / THE IDEA

Attention attracts attention.

Under the Barabási–Albert rule, a newcomer chooses existing nodes with probability proportional to their current degree. Early nodes have an advantage, while individual outcomes remain random.

02 / THE MISSING PIECE

A disconnected universe.

Starting from a connected seed, standard BA growth with at least one link per newcomer stays connected. Our Marvel roster has an island and 17 isolates. That discrepancy deserves an explanation.

03 / THE NEXT QUESTION

What else shapes a link?

Article length, shared storylines, publication history, and editing conventions are candidate influences. Testing them needs information beyond the edge list.

02 / COMPUTED

Count the neighbours.

The game builds the simple undirected graph from the bundled data. It compares each non-isolate's degree with the arithmetic mean degree of its neighbours. The report's edge-endpoint mean weights nodes by degree.

Isolates have no neighbour average and are excluded from the 250 / 286 paradox fraction.

03 / ILLUSTRATIVE

A sketch is not a test.

The reference curves and the game's preset shuffle gauges explain concepts. They do not supply fitted models, randomization ensembles, or confidence intervals.

We make no power-law significance claim or causal claim about superhero teams from these sketches.
