01 / THE QUESTION

EXERCISE 4.13 · GO NUTS

No philosopher
is an island.

We asked two questions: does counting repeated links change the communities, and how much can we erase before the connected world comes apart?

Louvain puts every philosopher in one group. A page’s links need not respect that assignment. Select Aristotle in the atlas to see the communities at the other ends of his connections; then change the weighting to see whether his seat changes.

The unweighted search finds 9 communities with modularity 0.502. Six graphs with the same degree sequence, rewired by double-edge swaps, average 0.220. The links contain more clustering than that baseline, but a partition cannot by itself name a historical tradition.

READ THE MAP

A reference is a trace.

A Wikipedia link may record influence, disagreement, a biography, or editorial habit. A thick link means more link occurrences in this dataset. It does not measure agreement.

Group names use prominent members as landmarks. They are invitations to investigate, rather than labels supplied by the algorithm.

    WHERE WE CALL IT A BREAK

    We define the breaking point as the largest component falling below half the original 1,374 people. At α ≈ 0.169585, tightening the filter takes it from 699 to 677. Five ties share that cutoff, but Confucius—Voltaire is the only one joining the 22-node detached group to the giant. That group includes Confucius, Laozi, Han Fei, and Zhu Xi: several East Asian traditions, not one school.

    A cutoff does not establish one philosopher as the cause. The links removed at that threshold form a cut between components; their endpoints show where this particular map comes apart.

    05 / THE METHOD

    REPRODUCIBLE, WITH LIMITS

    A partition is a lens.

    The data

    The course’s 15 September 2026 release has 1,444 pages and 11,135 directed links. We collapse direction, remove self-links, and retain the 1,374-node giant with 9,139 unique ties. The other 70 pages are outside this map.

    The communities

    Seeded Louvain (2026), resolution 1, with local moving and repeated aggregation. Ten seeds give modularity 0.499–0.507. Colors represent one run, not historical schools. NMI uses arithmetic entropy normalization.

    The filter

    At degree k and strength s, a link of weight w has endpoint significance (1 − w/s)k − 1. We keep it when either endpoint is below α. For degree one we assign 1: its only tie survives when significant at the other end. Components include isolates.

    How to read the comparison and the layout

    Weighted Louvain uses the sum of observed weights in the two directions. Colors are aligned by maximum overlap. A “mover” is outside its matched group, which depends on that alignment; NMI is independent of group names. Modularity scores on differently weighted graphs are not directly comparable.

    Positions are a deterministic visual aid computed from the α = 0.2 backbone, then held fixed. Distances are not dates or philosophical similarity scores. Six degree-preserving nulls provide a baseline, rather than a significance test. The dataset preserves the course roster’s inclusion and Wikipedia’s editorial biases.
