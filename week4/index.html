01 / THE FINDING

UNWEIGHTED GIANT COMPONENT

Links remember schools.

The largest detected group contains 193 names; the next two contain 191 and 189. That is not a taxonomy of philosophy. It is a map of who cites whom on one frozen day.

Each directed reference becomes one undirected, unweighted tie, and we analyse only the 1,374-node giant component. Louvain performs repeated local-moving and aggregation phases, unpacks the super-node assignments back to original pages, and uses reproducible shuffled node orders.

Across ten seeds, modularity ranges from 0.450 to 0.469 (mean 0.460); pairwise same-community agreement averages 0.940. The result is useful as a question generator, not proof that the people inside a cluster belonged to one school.

FIELD NOTE / 04

Compared with a null.

Six degree-preserving double-edge-swap nulls use the same 1,374 nodes and degree sequence. Their mean modularity is 0.193; the observed seed-2026 result is 0.468. This is a contrast, not a p-value: the collection is curated and the null is a structural baseline.

02 / METHOD

PROVENANCE & LIMITS

A partition is a lens.

Snapshot: 15 September 2026, the course's seven lists of philosophers born before 1900. The raw TSV and generator are committed for reruns. The primary result is explicitly unweighted and undirected; reciprocal edge counts are retained only as optional metadata. We report ten seeds, stability, and degree-preserving null scores so another lens can be compared honestly.
