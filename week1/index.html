01 / THE FIRST DISPATCH

WEEK 1 · THE BOOTSTRAP EDITION

Who gets to be
the centre of a universe?

Imagine Wikipedia as a telephone exchange. Some lines never stop ringing. Others never ring at all. What does the wiring actually look like?

Here, each line is a character page in the course’s frozen Marvel roster. The calls are a metaphor for hyperlinks. An arrow from A to B means A’s article links to B. It measures a reference within this particular collection of pages; it does not tell us who is friends, who is stronger, or who would win a fight.

We loaded every character first, then added the directed links. That little order of operations matters: an edge list alone would erase all 17 isolates before the investigation even began.

OUR WORKING HYPOTHESIS

“The most mentioned hero must also have the most to say.”

A reasonable guess.
An excellent way to be wrong.

03 / THE ATTENTION ECONOMY

SAME PAGES. TWO VERY DIFFERENT ROLES.

Being mentioned ≠
doing the mentioning.

A few pages gather extraordinary attention.
Switch the axes to see how far the tail reaches.

THE TOP FIVE

Ties are ordered by page ID. These are hyperlink counts, not a popularity poll.

One arrow. Two editorial decisions.

An incoming link is written on somebody else’s page. An outgoing link is written on this page. Those are different processes, so their leaders need not match. A long article, a sprawling team history, or an editor’s linking habits could help explain a high out-degree—but the edge list alone cannot tell us which explanation is right.

The observed in-degree reaches 106, while out-degree stops at 28. A long tail is visible. We have not fitted or validated a power law: a suggestive log–log plot is a starting question, not a verdict.

↳ A NOTE ABOUT ZERO

Zero cannot appear on a logarithmic axis. The log–log view omits zero-degree values and reports them explicitly. We do not silently shift every degree by one.

05 / A SMALL THOUGHT EXPERIMENT

YOU CONTROL THE COUNTERFACTUAL

What if the star
left the universe?

Spider-Man gets the most incoming links. Does that make him the load-bearing wall?

Remove the most linked-to characters, one at a time, and recalculate the largest weakly connected component. We keep the ranking fixed at its original in-degree. All links touching a removed page disappear with it.

The surprise: remove Spider-Man and the mainland shrinks from 277 to 271 pages. Beyond Spider-Man himself, 5 more pages detach. The broader web holds together, but some neighbours depend entirely on that one connection.

This is a structural experiment on hyperlinks, not a prediction about Marvel. A well-connected star can still be replaceable if other routes hold the web together.

03 / THE LIMITS

A map has a point of view.

Category membership and Wikipedia’s editorial choices shape the network. Coordinates are a deterministic drawing, not physical distance or measured communities. Small components are arranged separately for visibility.

We did not recrawl the live API or test a power-law model. Claims about why editors link characters remain hypotheses for a later text-based investigation.
