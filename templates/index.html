01 / THE FIRST FIELD REPORT

WEEK 1 · THE BOOTSTRAP EDITION · 7 MIN READ

Who gets to be
the centre of a universe?

We started with a suspiciously simple question: if everyone is a superhero, does everyone get a seat at the table?

Our table is Wikipedia. Each seat is a character page in the course’s frozen Marvel roster. An arrow from A to B means A’s article links to B. It measures a reference within this particular collection of pages; it does not tell us who is friends, who is stronger, or who would win a fight.

We loaded every character first, then added the directed links. That little order of operations matters: an edge list alone would erase all @@ISOLATE_COUNT@@ isolates before the investigation even began.

OUR WORKING HYPOTHESIS

“The most mentioned hero must also have the most to say.”

A reasonable guess.
An excellent way to be wrong.

THE TOP FIVE
@@RANKING@@

Ties are ordered by page ID. These are hyperlink counts, not a popularity poll.

One arrow. Two editorial decisions.

An incoming link is written on somebody else’s page. An outgoing link is written on this page. Those are different processes, so their leaders need not match. A long article, a sprawling team history, or an editor’s linking habits could help explain a high out-degree—but the edge list alone cannot tell us which explanation is right.

The observed in-degree reaches @@SPIDER_IN@@, while out-degree stops at @@OUT_MAX@@. A long tail is visible. We have not fitted or validated a power law: a suggestive log–log plot is a starting question, not a verdict.

What if the star
left the universe?

Spider-Man gets the most incoming links. Does that make him the load-bearing wall?

Remove the most linked-to characters, one at a time, and recalculate the largest weakly connected component. We keep the ranking fixed at its original in-degree. All links touching a removed page disappear with it.

The surprise: remove Spider-Man and the mainland shrinks from @@GIANT@@ to @@GIANT_WITHOUT_SPIDER@@ pages. Beyond Spider-Man himself, @@DETACHED_WITHOUT_SPIDER@@ more pages detach. The broader web holds together, but some neighbours depend entirely on that one connection.

This is a structural experiment on hyperlinks, not a prediction about Marvel. A well-connected star can still be replaceable if other routes hold the web together.

03 / THE LIMITS

A map has a point of view.

Category membership and Wikipedia’s editorial choices shape the network. Coordinates are a deterministic drawing, not physical distance or measured communities. Small components are arranged separately for visibility.

We did not recrawl the live API or test a power-law model. Claims about why editors link characters remain hypotheses for a later text-based investigation.
