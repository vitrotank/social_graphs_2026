01 / DOCUMENTS AS VECTORS

303 ARTICLES · 702,250 TOKENS

When counts learn
to discount.

Raw word counts reward frequent words until boring words dominate every comparison. TF-IDF fixes this by asking how unusual a word is across the full corpus.

Because the words Marvel and Comics appear on all 303 character articles, their inverse document frequency is $\log(303/303) = 0$. Like the English stopword the, they receive zero weight automatically, without needing an arbitrary human-curated stopword list.

The Name Trap: Yet TF-IDF brings its own blind spot. It rewards rare words, and surnames are exceedingly rare. Storm's closest textual neighbor is the Human Torch simply because his secret identity is Johnny Storm. Similarly, Emma Frost matches Jack Frost. TF-IDF cannot distinguish a character's family name from the semantic meaning of weather or ice. Telling those matches apart requires looking at text, not just coordinates.

COSINE SIMILARITY

Direction, not magnitude:

Euclidean distance is distorted by document length. Cosine similarity compares the angle between vectors:

cos(θ) = (A · B) / (||A|| ||B||)

Multiplying a document vector by 10 changes its length, but leaves its angle and cosine similarity completely unchanged.

02 / WORDS AND THEIR CONTEXTS

PPMI VS. SKIP-GRAM WORD2VEC

From co-occurrence
to prediction.

To represent words rather than documents, we turn to the distributional hypothesis: words that occur in similar surroundings have related meanings.

PPMI (Pointwise Mutual Information): In a raw co-occurrence matrix, every word's neighbors are dominated by function words (and, with, for). PPMI corrects this by dividing joint probabilities by marginal probabilities, revealing genuine associations.

Word2Vec: Skip-gram turns context into a prediction task, learning dense 100-dimensional vectors via negative sampling. Instead of computing a massive softmax across all 9,526 vocabulary words for every token, negative sampling turns training into efficient binary logistic regressions against $k$ noise words.

03 / THE MARVEL MAP

SCIENCE (−) VS. MAGIC (+) AXIS

Where does an axis
get its meaning?

Following Kozlowski, Taddy, and Evans (2019, The Geometry of Culture), we built a semantic axis by subtracting the average vector of science words from magic words. Projecting characters onto this axis reveals how the underlying corpus reshapes reality.

ONE CRITICAL LIMITATION

Polysemy and Static Vectors

Static word embeddings give each token exactly one point in space. The word bank merges river banks and financial institutions; the word cable merges electrical cords with a mutant warrior. Contextual models (like next week's Transformers) are needed to give words distinct coordinates per sentence.

05 / THE METHOD

REPRODUCIBLE, WITH LIMITS

A vector is a direction.

Dataset, Word2Vec parameters and vector space
THE ROSTER303 character articles
THE CORPUS702,250 word tokens
VOCABULARY9,526 unique words
DENSE VECTORS100 dimensions

The data

The 26 August 2026 archive contains all 303 character articles. We tokenized 34,341 sentences and 702,250 running words. Words occurring fewer than 5 times were removed, yielding a core vocabulary of 9,526 terms.

The model

Word2Vec Skip-gram trains on moving context windows (w = 5), optimizing dot products with negative sampling (k = 5) and single-worker reproducibility. Open-web baseline uses glove-wiki-gigaword-100.

The limits

Static vectors assign one coordinate per word, conflating polysemous senses. Antonyms appear close when sharing surrounding contexts; names introduce lexical coincidences in TF-IDF. Our claims describe this specific Wikipedia corpus rather than Marvel lore.

How to read the semantic axes and the game projection

Semantic axes follow Kozlowski et al. (2019): differences between centroid vectors for opposing concept lists (e.g. Science vs. Magic). Characters are projected onto this axis via cosine similarity and dot product.

In Vector Drift, 2D coordinates are a PCA projection of the 100-dimensional space onto the principal components of the mission characters. Vector addition (+ word) translates coordinates along the trained semantic direction.
